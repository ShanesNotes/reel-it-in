# Show Bible

## Premise

Reel It In is a weekly father-son conversation about AI.

The show is not trying to cover every AI headline. It filters what matters and explains why in plain English.

## Hosts

**Shane**

- follows the news and technology
- brings context and technical translation
- risks getting too deep, too fast

**Dad**

- asks the normal-person questions
- notices when the conversation has become jargon
- pushes for practical meaning
- represents the listener in the room

## Format Engine

Dad's line is the engine: "I will reel you in when you get too nerdy."

That means every segment has a natural rhythm:

1. Shane tees up what happened.
2. Dad reacts or asks the obvious question.
3. Shane translates.
4. Together they land on "so what?"

## Episode Shape

### Act I: The Setup

Short opening. Who we are, what the episode is about, and why this week's conversation matters.

### Act II: This Week

Three to five AI stories. Each story needs:

- source
- plain-English headline
- what happened
- why it matters
- one question Dad might ask

### Act III: The Conversation

Loose sparks, not scripts. A spark is a strong question or framing device that lets the hosts explore without reading.

Examples:

- Is anybody home in there?
- Whose answers are they?
- Are people treating AI like an oracle?
- Where does intelligence live when inference moves closer to us?

### Act IV: The Archive

The episode gets saved with source links, notes, and production metadata so the show becomes a durable shelf over time.

## What Makes A Good Episode

- Dad understands why each story matters by the end.
- Shane translates without flattening the topic.
- The conversation has room for surprise.
- The show avoids both hype and reflexive dismissal.
- The edit has at least a few moments worth clipping.

## What To Avoid

- fake headlines
- reading a script
- over-prepping Dad
- treating mythology/oracle language as literal claims
- explaining model mechanics before the listener knows why they care
