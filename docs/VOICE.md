# Voice

## North Star

Plain English with taste.

The show should sound like a smart friend at the kitchen table, not a white paper and not a reaction channel.

## Core Rules

- Use sentence case for headlines and prose.
- Translate jargon before it accumulates.
- Ask "so what?" constantly.
- Prefer questions over declarations in the conversation segment.
- Let humor undercut grandiosity.
- Keep Dad's genuine curiosity intact.

## The Dad Test

Before a story earns a spot, ask:

- Would Dad know why this matters by the end?
- Can the headline be said out loud without sounding like a press release?
- Is there a human consequence?
- Is there a practical or philosophical hook?

If not, it is probably noise.

## News Rewrite Formula

Press headline:

> Company X announces expanded autonomous agent framework for enterprise deployment.

Reel It In version:

> A company wants AI to do more work on its own inside offices.

Then answer:

> Why it matters: this is where AI stops being a chatbot and starts becoming part of how work gets assigned, checked, and measured.

## Sparks

Sparks are doors, not lectures.

A good spark:

- is one question or tension
- is glanceable during recording
- invites Dad in
- has room for disagreement
- does not require technical background to start

Bad spark:

> The implications of transformer-based architectures on democratic information systems.

Better spark:

> Whose answers are they?

## Myth And Oracle Language

The mythology thread is powerful because people already use AI like an advisor, mirror, oracle, confessional, or ritual object.

Use that language as metaphor. Keep the show grounded in behavior, incentives, history, and practical consequences.

## Microcopy Patterns

- `Not all the AI news - just what matters, and why.`
- `No jargon survives the table.`
- `The shelf is empty - for now.`
- `Drop this week's story.`
- `Mark covered`
- `Wildcard`
- `Live Mode`
- `On Air`
